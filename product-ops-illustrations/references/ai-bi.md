# AI, business intelligence, and mining applications

Keep the product's actual capability and stage of development explicit. An idea, prototype, pilot, and production system have different evidential status. Represent only supplied functions and outcomes.

## AI products

Preserve the stated request/input, processing, generated output, human checkpoint, and release or action. Keep proposed content visibly upstream of approval when approval is required. Model output is not automatically a correct answer, a business decision, or an executed control action. Do not add memory, autonomous tools, training, retrieval, or real-time behavior unless supported.

An agent, a predictive model, a deterministic rule, and a BI report perform different roles. Use the source's terms; if a needed distinction is absent and changes the workflow, ask. If not, use a neutral processing block instead of assuming a specific architecture.

## Business intelligence

Preserve data origin, transformation, validation, semantic/business meaning, reporting, and human decision steps when supplied. A report exposes information; it does not inherently schedule a train or operate a plant. Distinguish ingestion from validation, and technical data models from predictive AI models.

Use simple labelled sheets, containers, lenses, or views rather than a fabricated realistic dashboard. Avoid fake charts, percentages, alert counts, or claimed freshness. Preserve stated refresh cadence and distinguish observed values from forecasts when those distinctions matter. Use dashed teal paths for information when juxtaposed with material flow.

## Combined mining and digital workflows

Read [mining](mining.md) too. Start from the physical operation and identify exactly where information originates. Draw the AI/BI product as a separate supporting path, then connect its output to the named planner, analyst, supervisor, or other decision-maker.

Use solid amber arrows for physical movement and dashed teal arrows for information, with short labels "Material" and "Information" when both coexist. A teal product output should terminate at a decision or review when that is the documented behavior. Only show a subsequent physical/control action if supplied; never imply autonomous dispatch or closed-loop control from "decision support" alone.

For example, when the brief says stockpile inventory and a rail plan feed a BI view used by a planner, show those two information inputs, the view, and the planner. Do not infer sensors, forecast models, optimized schedules, or automatic train control.

## Evidence and wording

Copy required labels accurately. If the source offers no quantified benefit, use an observable structural change (a shared view, a review step, a consolidated handoff) rather than a performance claim. Before/after illustrations compare supplied workflows; do not make the after state appear entirely autonomous or error-free.
