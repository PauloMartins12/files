# Mining processes and operational handoffs

Use the provided process, its vocabulary, and its boundaries. A pit-to-port chain is a possible scope, not a universal route. A conceptual stage can be named without asserting its internal equipment. Recognizable silhouettes are acceptable; detailed plant machinery implies a process and requires source support.

## What to preserve

- Pit: the supplied extraction, movement, destination, and ownership relationships. Do not add a fleet, mining method, grade-control step, or dispatch system merely because it is plausible.
- Plant: supplied inputs, named processing stages, outputs, recycle loops, and constraints. Do not substitute one commodity's route for another or infer crushing, washing, flotation, smelting, or beneficiation from the word "plant".
- Rail: supplied loading, stockpile, train, destination, dispatch, and handover relationships. Preserve whether a step is planning, execution, or confirmation.
- Port: supplied receipt, storage, reclaim, loading, and shipment boundaries. Do not invent a berth, queue, shiploader configuration, or direct plant-to-ship connection.

Keep material names as supplied. Ore, concentrate, product, waste, and stockpile inventory are not interchangeable. Preserve branch direction, handoffs, waiting points, and the distinction between a plan and a completed movement. Never attach fabricated throughput, grade, savings, availability, or emissions figures.

## Decide whether clarification is needed

If asked to draw a site's exact plant process with only "processing plant" supplied, ask for its actual stage sequence or source diagram. If asked for a conceptual chain already specified as pit → plant → rail → port, draw those four stages without inventing internals or asking for ore type unnecessarily.

For a handoff, identify what is transferred, from whom, to whom, and how receipt or acceptance occurs when specified. Show a problem at that interface rather than inventing an unrelated broken machine.

## Visual treatment

Use broad recognizable forms: terraced pit, simple plant enclosure, stockpile, rail wagons, berth or vessel only when they belong to the supplied boundary. These are conceptual forms, not equipment specifications. A human character can exchange a handoff record, inspect a named decision point, compare represented information, or gesture toward an illustrative route; do not imply a small character physically moves trains or controls machinery without a clear metaphorical treatment. Follow the user's character and outfit assignments; if unspecified, use the default sheet and [uniform references](character-and-uniforms.md) rather than inferring clothing from role.

Show physical flow as solid amber arrows. If operational information appears too, use dashed teal paths and a short key. Separate information about a stockpile from the material stockpile itself. See [AI and BI](ai-bi.md) for decision support overlays.
