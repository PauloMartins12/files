# Image acceptance and correction

Inspect the generated image itself, not just its prompt. Check metadata for format and dimensions where available, and visually inspect labels, paths, and identity.

## Must pass

1. **Meaning:** One intended takeaway; correct stage order, handoff, actor, and process boundary. No invented equipment, product functions, metrics, or source facts.
2. **Flow:** Arrow endpoints are unambiguous. Mixed physical and information paths use both distinct line styles and a short key. A required review is not bypassed; decision support does not look like autonomous control.
3. **Domain:** Material names are preserved. The mine's actual route is not replaced by a generic plant route. AI output, human decision, report, and control action remain distinct.
4. **Language:** Labels match the supplied or approved short English copy, are spelled correctly, and are readable at the intended placement. No ghost text, repeated labels, unintended title, or sample-specific text carried over.
5. **People and workwear:** Check each named actor against the chosen sheet, then check the user's character-to-uniform assignment separately. Any of Paulo, Kayne, Brett, or Phill may wear the Rio Tinto navy jacket, navy polo, or yellow/navy operations shirt; use the default sheet assignment only when unspecified. When shown together, Brett is shortest, Phill tallest, and Paulo and Kayne have similar middle heights. Preserve natural hands, readable expressions, and appearance across related scenes. Check jacket zip, polo collar and placket, yellow/navy operations shirt, and one small red `RioTinto` wordmark on the wearer's left chest when visible at legible scale. Check garment color and absence of unsupported PPE. The action supports the concept and does not hide important information.
6. **Composition:** Reusable mining cutouts have true transparency and no scenery. Where mining subjects appear, pit benches, plant structure, loaded ore wagons, and port assets are recognizable at diagram size; no unsupported route or machine is added. Arrows, flowchart shapes, records, and icons use the rough hand-drawn notation, with open arrowheads and correct flow semantics. Requested pack and version are recognizable without copying an example. Keep white background, sparse charcoal/teal/amber diagram accents, natural character colors, large blank areas, no dense slide framing or fake interface. In Realist, use natural adult detail and restrained shading. In Cartoon, simplify faces and fabric and redraw equipment icons, records, arrows, and key in the same loose ink treatment. No copied cartoon dialogue, signature, or watermark.
7. **Delivery:** Actual PNG for illustrated outputs; isolated asset PNGs retain alpha; editable notation SVGs parse as XML. For diagrams: default 16:9 format (allow at most one pixel of raster rounding: absolute difference between height and width × 9 / 16 ≤ 1); one image per intended illustration; ordered descriptive names; saved paths work; original files preserved. If a user specifies exact pixels, check those separately and disclose any mismatch.

## Proportionate correction

- One label error: edit that label only, preserving other content.
- Several label errors: reduce copy and regenerate, without losing essential terminology.
- Unsupported or wrong connection: fix the structure, not just arrow decoration.
- Clutter: remove nonessential objects and labels; never remove a critical gate.
- Character drift: reuse the chosen named team sheet and relevant uniform photo, correcting the face, relative height, proportions, or garment while preserving the process.
- Wrong dimensions: request an image-tool correction to the required canvas; do not silently stretch, crop, or distort a process.
- An edit changes unrelated details: correct it against the original and inspect both images.

Reinspect a correction. After two corrections fail to resolve the same issue, state the remaining limitation and propose a simpler version. Do not mark an unresolved item as passed. Do not add extra images, tests, or unrelated style revisions once the brief passes.
