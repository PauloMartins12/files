# Visual system and human characters

## Shared visual language

- Finished diagram background: pure white, without paper grain, gradients, or scene-wide texture. Reusable mining PNG cutouts have actual transparent alpha outside the subject; no sky, road, horizon, or scene background.
- Primary line and text: charcoal `#263238`, thin and slightly imperfect, with clear contours. Arrows, flowchart shapes, records, icons, and legends follow [hand-drawn diagram notation](diagram-notation.md): slight wobble, sparse hatching, and open arrowheads.
- Teal `#168B87`: information, feedback, and system context.
- Amber `#E5A52A`: physical flow, movement, decisions, and selective emphasis.
- Characters use natural skin and hair colors, photo-guided navy blue and yellow workwear, and a small red Rio Tinto chest wordmark on the selected jacket, polo, or operations shirt when visible at a legible size. These colors need not match the diagram accents. Use restrained flat color and gentle local shading to give faces, hands, and clothing form.
- Main scene occupies roughly 40–60% of the canvas with at least 35% clear whitespace. Use one or two main visual groupings, avoiding dense grids and excessive arrows.
- Default 16:9 landscape PNG. The character reference sheet is an asset guide; its lineup is not a default illustration layout.
- Use 3–5 short English labels, preferably 1–4 words each. Retain essential source terminology even when longer. Encode secondary facts through relationships or the accompanying explanation rather than extra image copy. A minimal extra label is acceptable when essential to the gate or meaning. Use neat hand lettering with adequate contrast. Quote exact diagram labels in the prompt; specify any source-backed garment wordmark separately.

Where physical and information flows coexist, reinforce color with line style and a short key. Do not rely on color alone. Yellow clothing does not denote material flow; only the styled paths carry that meaning.

## Human ensemble

Inspect the chosen [Realist sheet](../assets/character/realist-team-reference.png) or [Cartoon sheet](../assets/character/cartoon-team-reference.png), then follow [character and uniform guidance](character-and-uniforms.md). The user can assign any of the three Rio Tinto outfits to any named person; the default and alternate sheets show examples. Keep Paulo, Kayne, Brett, and Phill recognizable and their selected outfits consistent. Brett is shortest, Phill tallest; Paulo and Kayne have similar middle heights when standing together. Use believable adult proportions, recognizable facial features, natural hands, and expressive gestures. Avoid geometric bodies, visor faces, stick limbs, and oversized mascot heads.

**Realist:** Natural faces and hands, restrained local shading on people, and expressive rough-ink diagram notation. When the brief supports mining assets, use the recognizable detailed transparent [cutouts](mining-asset-library.md) as visual references rather than empty schematic stand-ins. Use the nine existing calibration images under `assets/templates/<pack>/`.

**Cartoon:** Fewer facial and fabric marks, loose thin ink contours, sparse flat fills, and generous white space. Use the same recognizable mining equipment silhouettes at suitable detail, while drawing notation and character features more simply. Redraw icons and arrowheads in the same hand-inked treatment rather than placing simplified people next to rigid vector symbols. Use the nine counterparts under `assets/templates/cartoon/<pack>/`. Preserve process relationships and line-style distinctions.

The supplied cartoon examples inform broad visual qualities; do not reproduce their compositions, jokes, lettering content, signatures, or watermarks in generated work.

## Actions and scale

Use characters to clarify a supplied responsibility: exchange a handoff record, inspect a checkpoint, compare information, confirm a proposed result, or pass an approved item. One figure is usually enough; two can clarify a transfer or a comparison. Scale people so their gestures and uniforms remain readable without hiding the process.

Do not invent an operational responsibility simply to give a character a task. An illustrative gesture toward a route does not establish that the person operates the depicted equipment. Keep physical scale and metaphor clear.

## Adaptation

Keep linework, human proportions, selected character identity, outfit fidelity, lettering, and whitespace stable across packs. Adapt objects, scene organization, arrow paths, gestures, and label placement to the supplied concept. The calibration images are examples of visual treatment, not fact sources or layout masters. Explicit user choices can override the default cast or wardrobe.
